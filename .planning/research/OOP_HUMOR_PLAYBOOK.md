# OOP Humor Playbook — generating in Nikhil's taste, inside this game's voice

_Run 10 deliverable. Companion to `OOP_HUMOR_CATALOG.md`. This is the brief a later humor pass (Run 05) reads INSTEAD of raw OOP examples._

## The one-paragraph thesis

Out-of-Pocket's humor has three layers: **targets** (rules, payers, bureaucracy, himself — never patients), **materials** (real artifacts, real regulations, real absurd endpoints, explained accurately before being mocked), and **delivery** (captions, cappers, escalating bits, performed to an audience). The first two layers transfer to PrivacyQuest almost losslessly. The third layer is exactly what `VOICE_AND_HUMOR.md` bans — a performer's register, prepared sentences, announced punchlines. **So the transfer rule is: steal his targets and materials, replace his delivery with the game's grounded channels — situations, artifacts, silence, and UI surfaces that can be "written at" (item descriptions, citation cards, loading screens) without a character performing.**

A second structural note: the game has voice channels OOP doesn't, and OOP's delivery *is* legal in some of them. The grounded-speech filter applies to **character mouths**. Item descriptions, loading screens, codex entries, and parody documents are *authored* surfaces — a written artifact in the world is allowed to be written. That's where his caption energy lives.

## The 7 devices that survive

### 1. Found-artifact-as-punchline ★ the best transfer
**How it works in OOP:** a screenshot of a true thing (CMS coverage language, a Reddit thread of patients swapping CPAP data, uBiome's internal memo calling sample-rerunning a "growth hack") presented with a flat caption. The artifact indicts itself; he just holds it up.
**Real examples:** the uBiome fraud exhibits (uBiome post); real EHR lab-format screenshots played as found-object horror (Why Are Docs Late).
**PrivacyQuest transfer:** put the artifact IN the room as an inspectable object. Nobody comments on it. The item description is flat and official — the deadpan is structural.
> *Inspectable tray on the IT Office fax machine:*
> **CONFIRMATION — TRANSMISSION OK — 14 PAGES**
> **RECIPIENT: ANTONIO'S PIZZERIA & GRILL**
> *Someone has written "NO" on it in pen. Nothing else.*

This is also the fix for OOP's meme-caption instinct: the game's equivalent of a caption is a sticky note, a pen annotation, a whiteboard correction — diegetic captions.

### 2. Weird-rule deadpan recitation
**How it works in OOP:** recite the actual rule, accurately, with a straight face; the absurdity self-carries (the Birthday Rule, the 2 Midnight Rule, "It is now an office visit").
**Real examples:** Silly Little Rules and More Weird Rules — both posts explain the true mechanism before any joke lands.
**PrivacyQuest transfer:** a tired character states the real rule flatly, in fragments, without noticing it's absurd. No commentary, no wink. The player supplies the laugh.
> **JOANNA:** Thirty days. Extendable once. There's a form for the extension.
> *[stamps something]*
> **JOANNA:** There's a form for the form. Anyway.
That last line sits right at the t-shirt-test line — if it reads as a prepared bit in context, cut to just the stamp and "Anyway." The device works with zero wit: the true rule IS the content.

### 3. Absurd literalization — as situation, not sentence
**How it works in OOP:** follow a rule to its dumbest true endpoint (the Sunshine Act putting a sad airport pretzel in a federal database; records access so painful "you'd probably rather be in the hospital").
**Real examples:** the pretzel (Silly Little Rules); disposable CPAP part requiring a doctor visit (Paying for Friends).
**PrivacyQuest transfer:** build the endpoint into the room and let the player find it. The game shows the dumbest true consequence; no one points at it.
> *ER Observation zone: the patient status board has every name covered by a privacy sticky note. One sticky note is on the floor. The scene's choice is what you do about it — the absurdity (a federal privacy regime defeated by adhesive) is never voiced.*

### 4. Self-implicating cautionary tale
**How it works in OOP:** he includes himself in the indictment before mocking anyone ("myself included," the Orgo-midterm fake notes aside, "I don't love that I know this"). It buys the right to be cynical.
**Real examples:** fake email jobs "(myself included)" (Why Don't Companies Say); "I don't love that I know this" (Fringe Behaviors).
**PrivacyQuest transfer:** already sanctioned — GAME_DESIGN_PRINCIPLES' "Cautionary Tale" archetype. The OOP refinement: the confession arrives in fragments and stops early, instead of arcing to a moral.
> **SAM:** I used to keep mine on a — *[gestures at the monitor edge]* — yeah. So.
> *[citation card, separate UI: "§164.308 — workforce security. Written passwords near workstations are the most common audit finding."]*

### 5. Analogy smuggling — botched on purpose
**How it works in OOP:** a mundane analogy carries the whole mechanism (healthcare pricing = a coffee shop where "the cash register is operating on Windows NT"; texting patients = texting someone you're flirting with). The analogy is the Trojan horse for comprehension — his core educational move and the game's, per CLAUDE.md.
**Real examples:** the coffee-shop bit (Why Are Docs Late); flirting = patient engagement (How To Text Patients).
**PrivacyQuest transfer:** the analogy survives ONLY in-character and imperfect. A polished extended analogy is a prepared sentence; a character reaching for one and half-losing it is speech.
> **SAM:** It's like a coat check. You get the ticket, the coat stays — no, wait. The ticket is the... *[beat]* You need the key thing. That's the point. Don't email the key thing.
The comprehension still smuggles through. The botch is what makes it legal.

### 6. Data-as-indictment (chart-as-punchline, stripped of the punchline)
**How it works in OOP:** the number does the emotional work — 129.5 tracked hours of care; billing taking 3x the time of care coordination; a $16M fine against a 78M-record breach.
**Real examples:** Laura Stratte's time-tracking charts (Time Toxicity); Anthem fine vs. breach scope (Change Healthcare).
**PrivacyQuest transfer:** the stat appears on an authored surface — citation card, codex entry, break-room poster — stated flat, no editorializing. This is also where the game's licensed editorial POV (IDENTITY_AUDIT: "the game should have opinions") lives safely: the opinion is in the *selection* of the stat, not in commentary on it.
> *Citation card after a breach scenario: "Most reported breaches are not hackers. They are lost, unencrypted laptops."*
> *Break Room poster, printed: "DAYS SINCE LAST MISDIRECTED FAX: 30" — the 30 crossed out in pen, "3" written over it.*
(The poster is device 1 and 6 shaking hands. That crossover — flat data on a defaced official artifact — may be the single most OOP-shaped thing this game can do without anyone speaking.)

### 7. Verdict compression → understatement and silence
**How it works in OOP:** two-word verdicts ("Very PE-core."), one-word register drops, the refusal to elaborate. The instinct is compression.
**Real examples:** "Very PE-core." (Pixel Tracking); "That shit is great" as the entire verdict on marriage (Marriage post).
**PrivacyQuest transfer:** the compression survives; the wit doesn't. A two-word OOP verdict becomes a one-word character reaction plus a beat of silence — which VOICE_AND_HUMOR rule 9 already declares the best line in the scene.
> *Player picks the wrong answer.*
> **NINA:** No.
> *[goes back to typing. Citation card slides in beside the dialogue.]*

## The devices that did NOT survive — and why

- **Escalating hypothetical** ("Everyone is pro-consumerism until I suggest a marketplace for organs"). Stand-up architecture — it needs a performer building rounds toward a reveal, which is precisely the announced-punchline mode the voice doc bans. **Narrow exception:** wrong-answer *choice options* may escalate in plausible badness ("I'll pull just her chart" → "I'll pull the chart and print a copy, easier"), because options are player-voiced hypotheticals, not performed bits. Use sparingly; if an option reads as a gag, it breaks quiz integrity.
- **Nested-acronym mockery as commentary** (MACRA-contains-CHIP, the TEFCA tumbleweed). The joke is meta — someone pointing at the jargon. A character mocking acronyms is a character announcing a punchline. **Environmental version only:** the acronyms can simply be present (a wall poster listing real programs, straight), and a character may fail to remember one ("There's an acronym. I don't — it doesn't matter."). The mockery is never voiced.
- **Mock-formal register undercut by crude slang** ("Co-Bruh," "deez nutz," "F*** it we ball"). Double ban: it's a joke-writer's whiplash move, and the register is unusable in a training context regardless. Nothing to salvage; the *scary-term-mundane-reveal* half of it survives inside device 2.
- **Pop-culture non-sequitur capper** ("If I die, bury me inside the Gucci Store."). Pure performed absurdism — the most quotable, most t-shirt device he has, and therefore the most banned. Any line optimized to be screenshot dies at rule 1.
- **Chart with a joke caption.** The caption is a prepared punchline. Strip the caption, keep the chart → device 6. Exception: diegetic captions (pen annotations by an in-world hand) → device 1.
- **Fourth-wall breaks.** OOP does them constantly; GAME_DESIGN_PRINCIPLES caps the whole game at one or two. The cap is already spent in design intent — do not add more under an OOP banner.

## Register guardrails (reconciling with VOICE_AND_HUMOR.md)

1. **His targets are the law here too:** rules, systems, vendors, bureaucracy, yourself. Never patients, never suffering. The one patient-gallows post that works (Time Toxicity) works because the patient herself is telling it — in-game, gallows humor may come only from a patient NPC's own mouth about their own situation, and gently.
2. **Accuracy before texture.** OOP recites the real rule correctly before mocking it, every time. Same order here: the citation card is always straight; the humor lives in the room around it.
3. **Cynicism must be answered.** "The fine is smaller than the breach" (Change Healthcare) and "HIPAA enforcement is toothless" are true-flavored and corrosive. Rule: an NPC may voice a cynical take ONLY if the game answers it — through the scenario's outcome, another character, or the finale's verdict logic. Unanswered systemic cynicism is off the table for a training-adjacent game.
4. **No winking fraud endorsement.** Marriage-gaming, "cowboying" your CPAP, "theoretically" framings — usable as *character quirk or villain pitch* (a hustler NPC the player scores against), never as neutral narration or a sympathetic NPC's advice.
5. **Do-not-port list** (from source posts, regardless of funniness): the suicidal-ideation throwaway (Why Are Docs Late), blood-rule mortality gag (More Weird Rules), organ-marketplace and diagnosis-betting bits as anything but villain material (Ethically Dubious), all profanity, "dat ass," innuendo ("nighttime measurements").
6. **Two boring lines per witty one** (voice doc rule 5) applies to OOP-derived material *hardest*, because his material arrives pre-witty. Budget: per scene, at most one OOP-shaped moment — artifact, recitation, or silence-verdict — surrounded by load-bearing boring speech.
7. **Verbatim ≠ shippable.** Nothing from the catalog lands in `gameData.json` as-is; generate in-device, then Nikhil green-lights. (Run brief commitment.)
