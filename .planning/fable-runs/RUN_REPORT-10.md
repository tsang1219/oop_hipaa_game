## The load-bearing finding

OOP's humor = targets + materials + delivery. Targets (systems, never patients) and materials (real rules recited accurately, real artifacts held up flat) transfer losslessly. Delivery (captions, cappers, performed escalation) is exactly what VOICE_AND_HUMOR.md bans. But the game has **authored surfaces OOP's register is legal on nearly unmodified** — item descriptions, loading screens, citation cards, defaced in-world documents. The playbook's channel-permission table (which device may live in which surface) is the piece I'd want Run 05 to obey most strictly: NPC mouths get only fragments; his written deadpan goes to written surfaces.

## The 3 funniest / riskiest — look here first

1. **Funniest transfer:** the Break Room poster — printed "DAYS SINCE LAST MISDIRECTED FAX: 30," the 30 crossed out in pen, "3" written over it. Found-artifact + data-as-indictment in one object, zero spoken lines. The most OOP-shaped thing the game can do inside its own voice rules.
2. **Funniest source bit:** the Sunshine Act's "saddest Auntie Anne's pretzel in JFK" ending up in a federal database — but note it survives only as *device* (absurd true endpoint of a disclosure rule), not as a line; the pretzel sentence itself is a t-shirt.
3. **Riskiest:** Change Healthcare's "the fine was smaller than the breach" cynicism. It's true, it's on-topic, it's his sharpest register — and it indicts the enforcement regime the game trains people for. Playbook rule: an NPC may say it only if the game *answers* it (scenario outcome, counter-character, or the audit-verdict finale). Unanswered, it's corrosive; answered, it might be the smartest beat in the game.
